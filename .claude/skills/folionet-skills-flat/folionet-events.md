# folionet-events
**Version:** v0 — built from folionet-icp, folionet-voice, and 02-channel-strategy. No partner list or confirmed dates yet — update once the Day 24 partner mapping and Month 2 dinner date are locked.

## What this skill does
Load this when planning, briefing, or promoting an in-person or co-hosted event. It defines the two event tiers, who to invite, how to pick a co-host, and the outreach sequence before and after — so events reinforce the same trust-and-authority positioning as the content, not a generic company mixer.

---

## WHY EVENTS AT ALL
Per `folionet-icp`, the #1 trigger for registration is a referral or a personal conversation with someone they trust — not an ad. Events exist to manufacture more of those trust-building conversations, concentrated with the right people, in person.

---

## TIER 1 — THE CEO DINNER (intimate, high-capital)

**Format:** 10–15 guests, private dinner, CEO hosts personally.
**Audience:** Profile 1 (El Empresario LATAM) primarily — business owners with $50K–$300K+ in accumulated capital, per the ICP doc. Invite by name, not by list.
**City:** Bogotá or Miami for the first one (per the plan) — both have concentrated Profile 1/2 audiences.
**Goal:** Not a pitch dinner. A room where the CEO talks candidly about LATAM capital, currency risk, and what he's built — CA comes up naturally, not as an agenda item.
**Format of the evening:**
1. Informal welcome, no name tags, no slides
2. CEO shares a short, personal point of view (5-10 min, conversational — pull tone from `folionet-voice`'s founder-authority pillar)
3. Open conversation, table format — not a presentation
4. One-pager left at each seat as a takeaway, never handed out mid-conversation like a sales leave-behind

**Invitation criteria (who gets on the list):**
- Matches Profile 1/2 CA-candidate signals from `folionet-icp` (capital, "I want someone to manage it" attitude, Director/VP/Founder title)
- Existing client referral preferred over cold invitation
- Cap at 15 — the format only works small

---

## TIER 2 — CO-HOSTED PANEL (broader reach, one influencer/partner)

**Format:** Panel or fireside chat, CEO + one co-host from the influencer/partner shortlist (Day 23–24 deliverable).
**Audience:** Broader than Tier 1 — can include Profile 2/3 and warm prospects, not just confirmed high-capital leads.
**Goal:** Borrow the co-host's trust and audience to introduce Folionet/CA to people who don't yet know the CEO directly.

**Co-host selection criteria:**
- Personal finance creator or business figure with real engagement in VE/AR/CO audiences (not just follower count — check the influencer shortlist's engagement-rate column once built)
- Audience overlaps with the ICP profiles — not a general finance audience with no LATAM concentration
- No conflicting financial product endorsements (compliance check — flag anything that could look like a competing or conflicting recommendation)

---

## PRE-EVENT OUTREACH SEQUENCE (both tiers)
1. **3-4 weeks out:** Personal invitation — not a mass email. For Tier 1, ideally a direct message or call from the CEO or an existing client referral. For Tier 2, co-host and Folionet cross-promote on LinkedIn.
2. **1 week out:** Confirmation + one-line reminder of what to expect (never "sales event" language — frame as a conversation/panel).
3. **1-2 days out:** Logistics reminder only (time, place, what's next).

## POST-EVENT FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE
1. **Within 24 hours:** Personal thank-you from the CEO (or co-host, for Tier 2) — not a mass email, individual notes for Tier 1.
2. **Day 2-3:** One-pager + discovery call link, framed as a continuation of the conversation, not a new ask. Pull exact phrasing from `folionet-pitch`.
3. **Day 7:** For anyone who hasn't booked a call, one soft touch — a relevant LinkedIn post tag or a personal note. Never a second hard ask.

---

## COMPLIANCE NOTE
In-person conversations carry the same FINRA rules as written content — no specific return promises, no implied guarantees, even in casual dinner conversation. Brief the CEO before Tier 1 dinners specifically, since informal settings are where compliance discipline slips easiest.
